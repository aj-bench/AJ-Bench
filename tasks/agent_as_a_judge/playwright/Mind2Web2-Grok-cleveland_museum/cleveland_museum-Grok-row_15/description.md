### Historical Context and Analysis
The Neolithic period in China (c. 8000–2000 BCE) saw the emergence of settled communities, agriculture, and advanced crafts. Cultures like Liangzhu (East China) specialized in jade rituals, Hongshan (Northeast) in shamanic symbols, and Majiayao (Northwest) in painted pottery. These objects were often buried with elites, indicating social stratification. Jade, prized for its hardness and symbolic purity, was abraded with sand, a laborious process. Pottery like the jar shows early brush use for decoration, foreshadowing later Chinese art forms.

Controversies include dating accuracy, as styles persisted into the Bronze Age, but CMA attributions are based on archaeological comparisons and analysis. Compared to other museums (e.g., Metropolitan Museum of Art), CMA's collection emphasizes ritual jades, contributing to global understanding of Neolithic China.
