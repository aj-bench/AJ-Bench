## Case 1: The Atrial Fibrillation Alert in Austin, Texas

In January of 2024, Austin resident Mike Gomez received an unexpected series of alerts on his Apple Watch.<sup>2</sup> As a man who felt "perfectly fine" and used his watch primarily for fitness tracking, he was surprised when he received an elevated heart rate warning. This was followed by a fifth notification that explicitly stated the device had detected atrial fibrillation and advised him to contact a health professional immediately.<sup>2</sup> Despite having no symptoms, Gomez heeded the warnings and went to the hospital. Doctors confirmed he was in "serious AFib" and informed him that had he not come in, he could have suffered a fatal stroke.<sup>2</sup>

## Case 2: The Silent Heart Block in Lawrenceville, New Jersey

In February 2024, Frank Haggerty, a 73-year-old retired accountant in Lawrenceville, New Jersey, was asleep when his Apple Watch began to vibrate and sound an alarm.<sup>3</sup> The device's low heart rate alert notified him that his pulse had dropped to a critically low 30 beats per minute, far below the normal range of 60 to 100 beats.<sup>3</sup> Despite the alarming reading, Haggerty felt no symptoms and "could run a marathon." His wife, alerted by the watch, took him to the emergency room. A doctor was shocked by his lack of symptoms and stated that he "might make it to the end of the hall." He was subsequently diagnosed with complete heart block, a condition caused by a fault in the heart's electrical system, and required an immediate pacemaker implantation.<sup>3</sup>

## Case 3: The Crash Detection in Indianapolis, Indiana

An anonymous man from Indianapolis credits his Apple Watch with saving his life after a severe car crash in October 2022.<sup>8</sup> While driving at 70 mph on a familiar road, a tire blew out, causing him to hit a telephone pole. The impact left him barely conscious and with his head on the steering wheel as smoke filled the car. In this acute, high-trauma situation, the new Apple Watch Series 8's Crash Detection feature, which is enabled by default, sent haptic feedback to his wrist and automatically initiated a call to 911. The device broadcast an audio message stating that a crash had occurred and provided his precise location.<sup>8</sup>

## Case 4: The High Heart Rate & Cancer Discovery in Flushing, New York

In October 2022, the mother of 12-year-old Imani Miles, a resident of Flushing, New York, noticed a constant beeping from her daughter's Apple Watch.<sup>11</sup> The device was repeatedly alerting Imani to an abnormally high heart rate, a reading that had never occurred before. Concerned, her mother took her to the hospital. Doctors initially diagnosed her with appendicitis, but during the appendectomy, they made a far more serious discovery: a rare neuroendocrine tumor on her appendix, which had already spread to other parts of her body. The tumor was later found to be cancerous.<sup>12</sup>

## Case 5: The Pacemaker-Worthy Heart Rate in Montecito, California

In July, Peter Moore of Montecito, California, a former tech executive, began to feel dizzy and light-headed.<sup>13</sup> Initially, he dismissed the symptoms, attributing them to fatigue and stopping at a Starbucks for a coffee. While sitting in the car, his Apple Watch began to "ping" him with a series of insistent notifications, warning him of a critically low heart rate that had dropped to 32 beats per minute.<sup>13</sup> He sent screenshots to his wife, who immediately drove him to the hospital. Upon arrival, a "crash team" surrounded him, ready with a defibrillator, as his heart rate had fallen even further to a dangerously low 24 BPM. He was diagnosed with a complete heart block and received an immediate pacemaker implant.<sup>15</sup>

## Case 6: The Kidney Failure Discovery in Tampa, Florida

In May 2018, 18-year-old Deanna Recktenwald of the Tampa area was in church when her Apple Watch gave her a notification to "seek medical attention" because her resting heart rate had reached 190 beats per minute.<sup>16</sup> The teen had a headache and was slightly short of breath, but she had no other symptoms. Her mother, a registered nurse, was initially unconcerned due to her daughter's healthy history. However, as the watch continued to provide the same warnings, her mother took her to urgent care, which then directed them to the emergency room. There, doctors determined that she was in kidney failure and that her kidneys were only functioning at 20%.<sup>17</sup>
