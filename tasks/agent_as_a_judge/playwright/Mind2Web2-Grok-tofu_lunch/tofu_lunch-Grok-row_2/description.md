This dish bakes at 400°F for about 25-30 minutes, flipping halfway, resulting in golden pieces perfect for meal prep. Reviewers on Allrecipes praise its ease and versatility, often adding it to stir-fries or salads for extra protein.

The Tofu Broccoli Stir-Fry builds on crisping techniques by coating tofu in cornstarch before frying in vegetable oil, then combining with broccoli, sliced water chestnuts, kung pao sauce, green onion, roasted peanuts, and a drizzle of toasted sesame oil. This creates a textural contrast with crunchy vegetables and nuts against the soft tofu interior. It's a one-pan wonder that cooks in under 20 minutes on high heat, stirring frequently to avoid burning. The kung pao sauce provides a spicy, nutty kick, making it a crowd-pleaser for those new to tofu, as noted in user comments highlighting its resemblance to restaurant-style dishes.

For a bolder flavor profile, General Tso's Tofu mimics the popular Chinese-American dish by cubing and coating tofu in cornstarch, frying in olive oil until crisp, and tossing in a sauce made from soy sauce, sesame oil, maple syrup, green onions, sambal oelek, rice wine vinegar, and minced garlic. The sauce is thickened with additional cornstarch for that glossy finish. Preparation involves a quick fry followed by simmering in the sauce for 5-10 minutes, yielding a sticky, sweet-spicy result. This recipe stands out for its balance of heat from sambal oelek and sweetness from maple syrup, with many reviewers appreciating its vegan adaptation of a classic without sacrificing taste.

Shifting to the dessert, the Chocolate-Banana Tofu Pudding uses silken tofu as a base for creaminess, blended with banana, confectioners' sugar, unsweetened cocoa powder, soy milk, and ground cinnamon. No cooking is required—just blend until smooth and chill for at least 30 minutes. The banana adds natural sweetness and thickness, while cinnamon enhances the chocolate notes, creating a pudding that's rich yet light. Users often describe it as indistinguishable from dairy-based versions, making it an excellent entry point for tofu skeptics in desserts.
