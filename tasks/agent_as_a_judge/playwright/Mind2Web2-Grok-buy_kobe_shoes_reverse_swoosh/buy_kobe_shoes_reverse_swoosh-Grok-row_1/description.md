The custom Nike Kobe 6 with reverse Swoosh represents a fusion of basketball heritage and streetwear innovation, drawing from Kobe Bryant's "Black Mamba" legacy and Travis Scott's disruptive aesthetic. While official Kobe 6 releases like the "Reverse Grinch" (a color-flipped version of the 2010 "Grinch" with standard Swoosh) dominate searches, customs flip the script—literally—by reversing the Swoosh, where the narrow tip points forward toward the toe instead of the traditional heelward orientation. This design tweak, popularized by Travis Scott's Nike collaborations since 2017, has inspired artists and fans to reimagine the Kobe 6 Protro silhouette.

#### Origins and Design Inspiration
The Kobe 6, originally designed by Eric Avar in 2010, features a textured snakeskin upper symbolizing Kobe's "Mamba" alter ego, dual-layer memory foam sockliner, Phylon midsole, and Zoom Air units for performance. Customs with reverse Swoosh often stem from Travis Scott's influence, whose Air Jordan 1 and Air Force 1 colabs introduced the flipped logo as a signature. For instance, a Reddit post in r/KobeReps discusses "Kobe 6 'Travis Scott' Customs," with users critiquing the execution but confirming the reverse Swoosh: comments like "If they had just painted the regular check backwards these could be cool" and "the reverse swoosh is a little too much" highlight mixed reception. The post likely includes gallery images of the mocha-toned upper with chunky, lifted reverse Swooshes, though details emphasize the divisive branding.

Historical context from Sneaker Freaker's "F**king With the Swoosh: A Brief History" shows reverse Swooshes predating Scott, appearing subtly on 1990s models like the Air Flare and Air Darwin, and later on basketball shoes like the PG 2 and Kobe AD NXT. Factory defects, such as upside-down Swooshes on Air Jordan 1s valued at \$146,000, add rarity appeal to altered designs. For Kobe 6 customs, this evolves into deliberate artistry, often in "Reverse Mocha" colorways blending browns, sails, and blacks.
