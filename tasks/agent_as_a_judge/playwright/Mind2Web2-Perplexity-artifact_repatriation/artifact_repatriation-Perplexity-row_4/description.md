## Archaeological Artifacts Repatriated from Europe and North America to Asian Countries (2018-2025)

Based on comprehensive research into repatriation cases from the specified timeframe, here are five documented archaeological artifacts that were returned from countries in Europe or North America to Asian countries:

### 1. Zidanku Silk Manuscripts (Volumes II and III)

**Artifact Name:** Zidanku Silk Manuscripts fragments - Wuxing Ling and Gongshou Zhan (fourth to third century BCE)[^1][^2][^3][^4]
**Country Returning:** United States (Smithsonian's National Museum of Asian Art, Washington, DC)
**Country Receiving:** China
**Year of Repatriation:** 2025 (ceremony held May 16, 2025)[^4]
**News Source:** https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/05/16/smithsonian-national-museum-asian-art-silk-manuscript-repatriation-china

These ancient silk manuscripts are the oldest known silk texts discovered in China and the only surviving manuscripts from the Warring States period. They were looted from a tomb near Changsha, Hunan Province in 1942 and smuggled to the US in 1946.[^2][^1]

### 2. Buddha Sheltered by the Serpent King Muchalinda

**Artifact Name:** 12th-century Buddha sculpture "Buddha Sheltered by the Serpent King Muchalinda"[^5][^6][^7]
**Country Returning:** United States (Art Institute of Chicago)
**Country Receiving:** Nepal
**Year of Repatriation:** 2025 (announced March 3, 2025)[^6][^5]
**News Source:** https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/03/07/art-institute-of-chicago-return-12th-century-buddha-sculpture-to-nepal

The sculpture was stolen from Guita Bahi in the Kathmandu Valley and had been on display at the Art Institute since 1997. The museum proactively contacted Nepal's embassy after provenance research revealed its stolen origins.[^5]

### 3. Phanom Rung Temple Pilaster Fragment

**Artifact Name:** 12th-century red sandstone pilaster depicting Krishna lifting Mount Govardhana[^8][^9][^10]
**Country Returning:** United States (Art Institute of Chicago)
**Country Receiving:** Thailand
**Year of Repatriation:** 2024 (announced June 18, 2024)[^10][^8]
**News Source:** https://news.artnet.com/art-world/art-institute-of-chicago-returns-a-12th-century-temple-column-to-thailand-2501806

This architectural fragment was identified as originating from the Phanom Rung Temple in northeast Thailand, marking the second artifact the Art Institute has returned to this same temple (the first being a Vishnu lintel in 1988).[^8]

### 4. Metropolitan Museum Khmer Sculptures

**Artifact Name:** Two ancient sculptures - "Golden Boy" (129cm statue) and "Kneeling Female" (43cm bronze sculpture)[^11][^12][^13][^14]
**Country Returning:** United States (Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York)
**Country Receiving:** Thailand
**Year of Repatriation:** 2024 (returned to Thailand in May 2024, welcomed home ceremony held May 21, 2024)[^13][^14]
**News Source:** https://washingtondc.thaiembassy.org/en/content/the-met-returns-two-ancient-statues-to-thailand

Both 11th-century statues were illegally trafficked from Thailand in 1975 by antiquities dealer Douglas Latchford and had been displayed at the Met from 1988 to 2023.[^14][^13]

### 5. Dutch Colonial Collection - Hindu-Buddhist Sculptures

**Artifact Name:** Four Hindu-Buddhist sculptures (statues of Bhairava, Nandi, Ganesha, and Brahma) plus 284 objects from Puputan Badung Collection[^15][^16]
**Country Returning:** Netherlands (Wereldmuseum, Amsterdam)
**Country Receiving:** Indonesia
**Year of Repatriation:** 2024 (officially returned September 20, 2024)[^16][^15]
**News Source:** https://www.government.nl/latest/news/2024/09/20/for-second-time-cultural-artefacts-to-be-returned-to-indonesia

The Hindu-Buddhist sculptures were taken from Java in the first half of the 19th century, while the Puputan Badung Collection objects were seized after a 1906 Dutch military campaign in southern Bali.[^15]

### Additional Notable Cases

**Thai Temple Lintels from San Francisco Asian Art Museum**
Two 9th-10th century sandstone lintels from Nong Hong Temple and Khao Lon Temple in northeastern Thailand were returned in 2021 after a three-year HSI investigation. They weighed approximately 1,500 pounds each and were valued at \$700,000.[^17][^18]

**Metropolitan Museum Cambodia Repatriations**
Fourteen Khmer sculptures were returned to Cambodia in July 2024, including a 10th-century goddess sandstone statue and 7th-century Buddha head, all linked to trafficking by Douglas Latchford.[^19][^12]

These repatriations reflect a growing global movement toward addressing historical injustices in museum collections and returning cultural heritage to countries of origin. The cases demonstrate increased collaboration between museums, law enforcement, and origin countries in identifying and returning looted artifacts.[^20][^21][^22]
