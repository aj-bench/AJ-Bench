## The Hobby Lobby Collection (United States to Iraq, 2018)
On May 2, 2018, U.S. authorities, spearheaded by ICE, returned 3,800 ancient artifacts to the Republic of Iraq.<sup>6</sup> This extensive collection, which included cuneiform tablets, cylinder seals, and clay bullae, was seized after being smuggled into the United States in a blatant violation of federal law.<sup>6</sup> The artifacts were destined for the arts-and-crafts retailer Hobby Lobby Stores, Inc., and had been falsely described on their shipping labels as "tile samples".<sup>6</sup> An investigation by ICE's Homeland Security Investigations revealed that Hobby Lobby employees had a "deliberate intent...to avoid using a customs broker for the transaction".<sup>6</sup> The repatriated items were from the ancient city of Irisagrig and encompassed a range of historical periods, including the Ur III and Old Babylonian periods (2100-1600 BCE), as well as Early Dynastic incantations.<sup>6</sup> This successful repatriation, the first of its kind to Iraq since 2015, demonstrates a powerful model for restitution based on the enforcement of modern legal statutes against illicit trafficking.<sup>6</sup>

## The British Museum's Afghan Collection (United Kingdom to Afghanistan, 2018)
In a significant return, the British Museum repatriated over 800 artifacts to the National Museum of Afghanistan in Kabul in 2018.<sup>9</sup> These treasures had been looted during Afghanistan's civil war and subsequently sold on black markets before being intercepted at customs at London's Heathrow airport.<sup>9</sup> The collection was vast, with 20 pieces being registered artifacts from the National Museum and the remaining 821 items having been illegally excavated and smuggled.<sup>9</sup> Among the returned objects was a Buddha statue that had once been part of the museum's original collection. A British citizen who had acquired the statue made the personal decision to return it to its homeland.<sup>9</sup>

## The Subhash Kapoor Trove (United States to Pakistan, 2021)
In 2021, U.S. authorities returned 104 artifacts to Pakistan from a vast trove seized from convicted art dealer Subhash Kapoor in New York.<sup>5</sup> Kapoor, who specialized in South and Central Asian antiques, had amassed approximately 2,500 artifacts valued at over $100 million, a large portion of which was believed to be looted or trafficked.<sup>5</sup> Among the items returned to Pakistan were a ceremonial casket and two bodhisattva statues, dating from the 2nd to 4th centuries A.D..<sup>5</sup>

## The Glasgow Museums' Collection (United Kingdom to India, 2023)
In January 2023, Glasgow Life Museums returned seven cultural heritage objects to India, marking a pivotal moment as the "first repatriation to India by a UK museums service".<sup>10</sup>

## The Metropolitan Museum's Khmer Antiquities (United States to Cambodia, 2024)
On July 3, 2024, Cambodia welcomed the return of 14 Khmer antiquities from the Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) in New York.<sup>7</sup> This collection included a highly significant 10th-century female goddess sculpture that had been removed from the ancient royal capital of Koh Ker.<sup>7</sup>
