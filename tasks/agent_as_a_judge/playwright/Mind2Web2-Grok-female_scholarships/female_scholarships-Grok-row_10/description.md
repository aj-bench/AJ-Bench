#### Background and Importance
Initiatives like these trace back to pioneers such as Dr. Anita Borg, whose legacy influenced programs aiming to dismantle barriers for women in tech. Organizations like the American Association of University Women (AAUW), founded in 1881, have awarded over \$135 million to more than 13,000 women scholars globally, with a focus on advancing equity. Similarly, the Society of Women Engineers (SWE), established in 1950, awards over \$1 million annually to support women in engineering and computer science. These programs not only provide financial relief—covering tuition, research, or living costs—but also offer networking, mentorship, and retreats to build community. For instance, recipients often gain access to professional development workshops, which can enhance career trajectories in academia, industry, or research.

Eligibility generally requires U.S. citizenship or permanent residency, full-time enrollment in a computer science or related graduate program (e.g., PhD or master's in CS, cybersecurity, or data science), a minimum GPA (often 3.3+), and demonstrated leadership or impact on women in tech. Applications typically include transcripts, essays on career goals and diversity contributions, letters of recommendation, and resumes highlighting publications, presentations, or volunteer work. Deadlines are cyclical, often in fall/winter for the following academic year, and awards are competitive, with panels reviewing for merit and alignment with gender equity goals.

#### Detailed Profiles of Selected Opportunities
Here are in-depth details on the five selected scholarships/fellowships, drawn from official guidelines and recent cycles. All are open to female U.S. citizens in U.S.-based CS graduate programs, with some extending to permanent residents.

1. **AAUW American Dissertation Fellowship**  
   This flagship program, part of AAUW's broader fellowships since 1888, targets women in the final year of doctoral studies. It supports dissertation completion in any field, including computer science, emphasizing resilience and contributions to gender issues or STEM. Eligibility: U.S. citizen/permanent resident; completed all coursework and exams; approved research proposal; first doctoral degree. Exclusions: prior AAUW national awards, concurrent applications to other AAUW programs. Process: Online application via AAUW portal, including narrative, recommendations, and transcripts. Review by expert panels in humanities, sciences, and social sciences. Funds (\$25,000) disbursed in two payments for tuition, living expenses, or research; taxable portions apply. Recent updates for 2025-2026 include a focus on diverse fields like CS, with notifications by April 15, 2026. URL: https://www.aauw.org/resources/programs/fellowships-grants/current-opportunities/american/dissertation/.

2. **P.E.O. Scholar Awards**  
   Established in 1991 by the Philanthropic Educational Organization (P.E.O.), this program aids women pursuing doctoral degrees with potential for societal impact. It covers fields like computer science, medicine, and law, with awards based on leadership and scholarly promise. Eligibility: U.S./Canadian citizen/permanent resident; within two years of graduation with at least one full academic year left; enrolled full-time at accredited U.S./Canadian institution; bachelor's degree minimum. Requires chapter nomination. Process: Nominations August 20-November 20; online application follows with essays, three recommendations, scholarly evidence (e.g., publications), and transcripts. Evaluated nondiscriminatorily by trustees. Funds (up to \$25,000) for study/research; one-time, merit-based. Notifications by May 1. Historical note: Over 1,000 awards given, emphasizing underrepresented fields. URL: https://www.peointernational.org/about-peo-scholar-awards.

3. **Society of Women Engineers (SWE) Scholarships**  
   SWE offers over 250 scholarships annually, many for graduate women in ABET-accredited programs including computer science and engineering technology. Named awards (e.g., Lydia I. Pickup Memorial, \$3,000) target specific needs like materials science or data processing. Eligibility: Identify as woman; enrolled/accepted in graduate program; U.S. institution preferred but open; membership encouraged. Process: Single online application for multiple awards; requires transcripts, essay on career goals, and recommendations. Review considers academics, leadership, and engineering/CS involvement. Funds (\$1,000-\$20,000) for tuition/books; disbursed directly. Typical graduate deadline: February 15, with awards announced in spring. SWE has awarded millions since 1950, supporting over 6,000 chapters globally. URL: https://swe.org/scholarships/.

4. **(ISC)² Women’s Cybersecurity Scholarships**  
   Administered by the Center for Cyber Safety and Education (charitable arm of ISC2), this targets women in cybersecurity—a core CS subfield—to address workforce gaps. Up to 10 awards annually. Eligibility: Female pursuing associate's, bachelor's, master's, or post-grad in cybersecurity/information assurance; 3.3 GPA; any citizenship but open to U.S.; enrolled fall 2025. Process: Online application with transcripts, recommendation, resume (max 3 pages), and essays on inspiration, goals, and "paying it forward" for women in tech. Scored on academics, passion, and need. Funds (\$1,000-\$5,000) for education; recipients get free Certified in Cybersecurity exam and ISC2 membership. Deadline: March 3, 2025; notifications June. Includes memorials like Samantha Jennings-Jones for public service. URL: https://iamcybersafe.org/s/graduate-scholarships.

5. **Graduate Women in Science (GWIS) National Fellowship Program**  
   GWIS, founded in 1921, funds hypothesis-driven research by women in sciences, explicitly including computer science and social sciences. Awards vary based on endowment. Eligibility: Woman identifying as such; degree from recognized institution; outstanding research promise; GWIS membership required; U.S./abroad institutions, open to U.S. citizens. No age/race restrictions. Process: Online application with proposal, budget, recommendations, and CV; focuses on research impact. Funds (up to \$10,000) for expenses like equipment/travel; \$75,000 total distributed in 2024-2025. Deadline: January 13, 2025, 5:00 PM ET. Emphasizes diverse STEM representation. URL: https://www.gwis.org/page/fellowship_program.
