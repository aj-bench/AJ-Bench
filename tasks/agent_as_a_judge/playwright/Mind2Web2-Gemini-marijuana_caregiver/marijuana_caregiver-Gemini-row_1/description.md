## II. Detailed State-by-State Regulatory Profiles

### Arizona

1. Patient Limit: A designated caregiver may assist a maximum of five qualifying patients.<sup>1</sup>  
2. Minimum Age: 21 years of age.<sup>1</sup>  
3. Residency Requirement: Yes, caregivers must be Arizona residents and provide proof of residency.<sup>44</sup>  
4. Cultivation Status: Permitted, with a significant geographic condition.  
5. Cultivation Limits: A caregiver may cultivate up to 12 marijuana plants per patient, but only if the patient lives more than 25 miles from the nearest licensed dispensary.<sup>1</sup> All cultivation must occur in an "enclosed, locked facility".<sup>21</sup>  

### California

1. Patient Limit: Conditional. Typically, a caregiver may only serve one patient. However, a caregiver is permitted to serve multiple patients if all of those patients and the caregiver reside in the same city or county.<sup>3</sup>  
2. Minimum Age: 18 years of age. An exception exists for emancipated minors or the parent of a minor patient who is also a qualified patient.<sup>5</sup>  
3. Residency Requirement: Yes, proof of residency within a California county is required to apply for a Medical Marijuana Identification Card (MMIC) for both the patient and the caregiver.<sup>30</sup>  
4. Cultivation Status: Permitted.  
5. Cultivation Limits: A caregiver may cultivate up to 6 mature or 12 immature marijuana plants per patient.<sup>3</sup>  

### Colorado

1. Patient Limit: A cultivating or transporting caregiver may serve no more than five patients. The state may grant a waiver for "exceptional circumstances" to exceed this limit.<sup>7</sup>  
2. Minimum Age: 18 years of age.<sup>28</sup>  
3. Residency Requirement: Yes, caregivers must be Colorado residents.<sup>28</sup>  
4. Cultivation Status: Permitted.  
5. Cultivation Limits: Tiered and complex. The baseline allowance is 6 plants per patient (with no more than three being mature, flowering plants).<sup>52</sup> However, a caregiver may cultivate a total of up to  
   36 plants for all their patients combined. If patients have received a physician recommendation for an "extended plant count" due to medical necessity, the caregiver can cultivate up to a maximum of 99 plants in total, but must register this higher plant count with the state.<sup>8</sup>  

### Florida

1. Patient Limit: A caregiver may serve only one qualified patient.<sup>10</sup> Narrow exceptions exist for parents or legal guardians of multiple minor patients, caregivers for multiple patients in a hospice program, and other specific circumstances.<sup>9</sup>  
2. Minimum Age: 21 years of age.<sup>9</sup>  
3. Residency Requirement: Yes, caregivers must be Florida residents, with specific provisions for seasonal residents who live in the state for at least 31 consecutive days per year.<sup>32</sup>  
4. Cultivation Status: Prohibited.  
5. Cultivation Limits: N/A. Home cultivation of marijuana by patients or caregivers is illegal in Florida. All medical cannabis must be procured from a state-licensed Medical Marijuana Treatment Center (MMTC).<sup>38</sup>  

### Michigan

1. Patient Limit: A primary caregiver may assist a maximum of five qualifying patients.<sup>22</sup>  
2. Minimum Age: 21 years of age.<sup>23</sup>  
3. Residency Requirement: Yes, caregivers must be Michigan residents and provide proof of residency, such as a valid state-issued driver's license or ID card.<sup>23</sup>  
4. Cultivation Status: Permitted.  
5. Cultivation Limits: A caregiver may cultivate up to 12 marijuana plants per patient. Cultivation must occur in an enclosed, locked facility.<sup>23</sup> If the caregiver is also a registered patient, they may cultivate an additional 12 plants for their own use, bringing the total potential plant count to  
   72 (12 plants x 5 patients + 12 plants for self).<sup>22</sup>  

### Oklahoma

1. Patient Limit: A licensed caregiver may be designated by up to five licensed patients.<sup>24</sup>  
2. Minimum Age: 18 years of age.<sup>29</sup>  
3. Residency Requirement: Yes, caregivers must provide proof of Oklahoma residency.<sup>24</sup>  
4. Cultivation Status: Permitted.  
5. Cultivation Limits: A caregiver may cultivate up to 6 mature marijuana plants and 6 seedlings for each patient they are designated to grow for.<sup>24</sup> All cultivation must be conducted on private property and must not be visible from any adjacent street.<sup>24</sup>  

### Oregon

1. Patient Limit: A patient may designate only one primary caregiver.<sup>12</sup>  
2. Minimum Age: 18 years of age.<sup>12</sup>  
3. Residency Requirement: Conditional. The patient is required to be an Oregon resident. However, the designated caregiver is not required to be an Oregon resident and may register using a valid government-issued ID from another state.<sup>11</sup>  
4. Cultivation Status: Permitted. However, if the caregiver is growing for the patient, they must be designated as the "Person Responsible for a Marijuana Grow Site" (PRMG) and must be at least 21 years old.<sup>12</sup>  
5. Cultivation Limits: The patient and their designated caregiver may jointly possess up to 6 mature marijuana plants and 12 immature plants.<sup>12</sup>  

### Pennsylvania

1. Patient Limit: Five patients. A caregiver is permitted to assist as many as five qualified patients in Pennsylvania.<sup>14</sup>  
2. Minimum Age: 21 years of age. The Pennsylvania Department of Health may grant an exception to this requirement upon written request.<sup>17</sup>  
3. Residency Requirement: Yes, caregivers must have a valid Pennsylvania driver's license or a state-issued ID card with a current address.<sup>17</sup>  
4. Cultivation Status: Prohibited.  
5. Cultivation Limits: N/A. Home cultivation is not permitted under current Pennsylvania law.<sup>15</sup> However, Senate Bill 869, introduced in 2023, proposes to authorize patients and their caregivers to cultivate up to six plants for personal medical use.<sup>18</sup>
