# Five Major Astronomy Instruments Operational 2005–2025 and Key Scientific Results

**Main Takeaway:**
Advanced facilities spanning radio interferometry, infrared to optical space telescopes, and precision astrometry missions have revolutionized our understanding of protoplanetary disks, galaxy evolution, exoplanets, and the Milky Way’s structure.

***

## 1. Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)

Instrument Type: Radio interferometer array
Year Operational: 2011
Operating Agency: ALMA Collaboration (ESO, NAOJ, NRAO)

Key Results:

- **First high-resolution image of a protoplanetary disk** around HL Tau, revealing concentric rings indicating planet formation in a ~1 Myr-old system.[^1]
- **Combined imaging of M87’s black hole shadow and jet base** at 3.5 mm wavelength via global mm-VLBI (GMVA + ALMA), showing the ring structure and emerging jet in a single observation.[^2]
- **Measurement of magnetic fields in the HD 142527 protoplanetary disk**, providing the first direct mapping of disk magnetism and its role in planet formation.[^3]

***

## 2. Herschel Space Observatory

Instrument Type: Space telescope (far-infrared/submillimetre)
Year Operational: May 2009–April 2013
Operating Agency: ESA (with NASA participation)

Key Results:

- **Detection of Earth-ocean-like water** in Comet Hartley 2, demonstrating that Kuiper Belt comets could have delivered a substantial fraction of Earth’s water.[^4]
- **Identification of “ultrared” dusty star-forming galaxies** at high redshift (up to z ≈ 6) and measurement of their luminosity functions, revealing rapid dust-obscured star formation in the early universe.[^5]
- **Confirmation of the galaxy “main sequence”** across cosmic time, showing that star-forming galaxies maintain a tight relation between stellar mass and star-formation rate from early epochs to the present.[^6]

***

## 3. Kepler Space Telescope

Instrument Type: Space telescope (optical photometer)
Year Operational: March 2009–October 2018
Operating Agency: NASA

Key Results:

- **Discovery of Kepler-10b**, the first confirmed rocky exoplanet, with a mass of ~3.7 M⊕ and radius of 1.47 R⊕.[^7]
- **Identification of Kepler-22b**, the first planet found in the habitable zone of a Sun-like star, with radius ~2.1 R⊕ and potential for liquid water.[^8]
- **Detection of over 2,700 confirmed exoplanets**, including Earth-size and super-Earth-size worlds, establishing exoplanetary statistics and demographics.[^9]

***

## 4. Gaia Spacecraft

Instrument Type: Space observatory (astrometry)
Year Operational: January 2014–March 2025
Operating Agency: ESA

Key Results:

- **Construction of a three-dimensional map of the Milky Way**, measuring positions and motions of over one billion stars to unprecedented precision.[^10]
- **Detection of hypervelocity stars and new black hole candidates**, revealed by precise stellar motions and gravitational effects in Gaia’s astrometric catalog.[^11]
- **Astrometric orbits for ~150,000 asteroids**, enabling refined models of solar system dynamics and discovery of possible asteroid moons.[^11]

***

## 5. James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)

Instrument Type: Space telescope (infrared)
Year Operational: July 2022–present
Operating Agencies: NASA, ESA, CSA

Key Results:

- **Discovery of the earliest known galaxy** (z ≈ 14), pushing the frontier of cosmic archeology by observing structures 280 Myr after the Big Bang.[^12]
- **Cosmic archeology of disk galaxies**, revealing the emergence of thin and thick stellar disks across billions of years and mapping galaxy evolution in infrared light.[^12]
- **Identification of water vapor in the ultra-hot gas giant WASP-18b’s atmosphere**, demonstrating JWST’s sensitivity to trace molecules at extreme temperatures (≈2,700 °C).[^13]

***
