Born in Gando, Burkina Faso, Kéré's journey from a village without electricity to international acclaim underscores his commitment to education and social activism. His projects often involve community participation, using clay, wood, and other vernacular materials to create structures that are both functional and environmentally harmonious.

Sir David Chipperfield, the 2023 laureate, is a British architect known for his restrained, contextual approach that respects historical settings while introducing modern elements. His work spans museums, galleries, and public buildings, emphasizing craftsmanship and subtlety. Chipperfield's designs often blend seamlessly with their surroundings, drawing on a deep understanding of place and history.

Riken Yamamoto, awarded in 2024, is a Japanese architect whose philosophy centers on blurring boundaries between public and private spaces to foster community interaction. His transparent, open designs challenge conventional housing and institutional architecture, promoting social connectivity in urban environments. Yamamoto's career, spanning over five decades, reflects influences from his travels and research into communal living.

#### Selected Architectural Projects

**Diébédo Francis Kéré (2022)**  
Kéré's designs prioritize low-cost, eco-friendly solutions that involve local communities, often in resource-scarce areas of Africa. His use of natural ventilation and earth-based materials combats extreme heat while promoting skill-building.  

- **Gando Primary School (Gando, Burkina Faso; completed 2001)**: This school was built using compressed earth bricks and a ventilated roof to provide natural cooling in a hot climate. It served as Kéré's diploma project and expanded access to education in his hometown, involving villagers in construction. The design features overhanging roofs for shade and passive cooling systems. [Project page with photos and description](https://www.archdaily.com/785955/primary-school-in-gando-kere-architecture).  
- **National Park of Mali (Bamako, Mali; completed 2010)**: Created for Mali's 50th independence anniversary, this urban park includes sports facilities, restaurants, and exhibition spaces integrated into the landscape. It features pedestrian paths, formal promenades, and sustainable elements like local stone and vegetation for erosion control. The project revitalized a neglected area into a communal hub. [Project page with photos and description](https://www.archdaily.com/167020/national-park-of-mali-kere-architecture).  

**Sir David Chipperfield (2023)**  
Chipperfield's architecture is characterized by minimalism, precise detailing, and sensitivity to site history. His projects often restore or complement existing structures, balancing heritage with contemporary needs.  

- **River and Rowing Museum (Henley-on-Thames, UK; completed 1997)**: Inspired by local timber barns, this museum houses rowing artifacts and Thames history exhibits. Elevated on stilts to mitigate flooding, it uses oak cladding and green roofs to blend with the riverside landscape. The design includes galleries for boats and interactive displays on rowing's cultural significance. [Project page with photos and description](https://davidchipperfield.com/projects/river-and-rowing-museum).  
- **Museum of Modern Literature (Marbach am Neckar, Germany; completed 2006)**: Perched on a plateau overlooking the Neckar Valley, this museum stores 20th-century German literature archives. Built with concrete and glass, it features a colonnade entrance and subterranean galleries for light-sensitive documents. The design draws on classical temple forms while providing panoramic views. [Project page with photos and description](https://www.archdaily.com/796411/museum-of-modern-literature-david-chipperfield-architects).  

**Riken Yamamoto (2024)**  
Yamamoto's work explores "transparency" in architecture, using glass and open layouts to encourage social interactions and dissolve barriers between spaces. His projects often serve as "thresholds" that connect individuals to their communities.  

- **Hiroshima Nishi Fire Station (Hiroshima, Japan; completed 2000)**: This fire station features a glass facade and central atrium, making internal activities visible to the public to demystify emergency services. It includes training areas around the atrium, promoting transparency and community engagement. The design uses steel and glass for durability and openness. [Project page with photos and description](https://www.archdaily.com/1014034/hiroshima-nishi-fire-station-riken-yamamoto).  
- **Yokosuka Museum of Art (Yokosuka, Japan; completed 2006)**: Embedded in a hillside, this museum integrates with nature through curved roofs and extensive glazing. It houses contemporary art collections with exhibition spaces, a library, and outdoor terraces. The design emphasizes views of Tokyo Bay, creating a dialogue between art, architecture, and landscape. [Project page with photos and description](https://www.archdaily.com/1014054/yokosuka-museum-of-art-riken-yamamoto).
