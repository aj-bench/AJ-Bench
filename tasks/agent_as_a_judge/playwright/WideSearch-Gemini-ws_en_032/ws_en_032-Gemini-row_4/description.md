# **II. Analysis of the Sydney Trains Network (T-Lines)**

## **T1 North Shore & Western Line**

The T1 North Shore & Western Line is one of the network's longest and most critical commuter arteries, serving a vast and diverse corridor stretching from the upper North Shore, across the Sydney Harbour Bridge, through the CBD, and deep into the western suburbs.<sup>13</sup>

**Route Description:** The line's route is a composite of several historic railway corridors. It commences at Berowra on the Main North Line, proceeding south through major centres like Hornsby and Chatswood on the North Shore Line.<sup>13</sup> After crossing the Harbour Bridge, it traverses the western limb of the City Circle (Wynyard, Town Hall) before passing through Central and Redfern. From Redfern, it runs express to Strathfield, joining the Main Suburban and then the Main Western Line through major hubs like Parramatta and Blacktown.<sup>13</sup> At Blacktown, the line bifurcates, with one branch continuing west to Emu Plains and the other heading north-west along the Richmond Line to Richmond.<sup>14</sup>

**Operational Characteristics:** As a primary radial line, its core function is to facilitate mass commuter movement between the residential areas of the North Shore and the west, and the employment hubs of North Sydney and the CBD. The line's extensive reach, with 56 stations, makes it a vital connector for a significant portion of Sydney's population. It typically operates with high frequencies, especially during peak periods.<sup>4</sup>

## **T2 Leppington & Inner West Line**

The T2 Leppington & Inner West Line is a complex service that connects Sydney's rapidly growing south-west and the established inner west with the CBD.<sup>16</sup> Its route structure reflects the amalgamation of several distinct service patterns into a single line.

**Route Description:** The T2 line operates via the City Circle, typically in an anti-clockwise direction, before heading west along the Main Suburban railway line.<sup>16</sup> It serves all stations through the inner west suburbs like Ashfield and Strathfield. At Granville, the line branches. The primary southern branch travels via the Old Main South and Main South lines through Liverpool and Glenfield, before switching to the newer South West Rail Link to terminate at Leppington.<sup>16</sup> A secondary branch continues west from Granville to Parramatta, while some shorter services terminate at Homebush.<sup>16</sup>

**Operational Characteristics:** This line serves a dual purpose. The Leppington branch provides a crucial link for the burgeoning population centres of the south-west, which saw significant expansion with the opening of the South West Rail Link in 2015\.<sup>4</sup> The inner west portion of the line provides high-frequency, all-stops local service to some of Sydney's most densely populated suburbs. The line's 36 stations underscore its broad coverage.

## **T3 Liverpool & Inner West Line**

The operational pattern of the T3 Liverpool & Inner West Line as of May 2025 is a direct consequence of the Sydney Metro conversion project. Its route has been specifically reconfigured to maintain a direct city link for commuters on the Liverpool branch of the former T3 Bankstown Line.<sup>11</sup>

**Route Description:** Originating at Liverpool, the T3 line travels along the Main South Line through Cabramatta to a junction near Sefton. From there, it proceeds via Regents Park and Berala to the major interchange at Lidcombe.<sup>11</sup> At Lidcombe, it joins the Main Suburban Line, sharing tracks with the T2 service through the inner west (Strathfield, Ashfield, Newtown) and into the City Circle.<sup>19</sup>

**Operational Characteristics:** The T3's primary function in its 2025 configuration is to provide a continuous, one-seat journey from the Liverpool area to the CBD, bypassing the closed section of the Bankstown Line. With 28 stations, it serves a mix of established south-western suburbs and the inner west. Its re-routing via Lidcombe is a critical, albeit temporary, measure to mitigate the disruption caused by the metro upgrade.<sup>11</sup>

## **T4 Eastern Suburbs & Illawarra Line**

The T4 Eastern Suburbs & Illawarra Line is a major north-south radial line, connecting the affluent eastern suburbs with the CBD and the extensive southern suburbs of the St George and Sutherland Shire areas.<sup>21</sup>

**Route Description:** The line begins at Bondi Junction in a deep underground station and travels west through the Eastern Suburbs Railway, with stations at key locations like Martin Place and Town Hall.<sup>21</sup> After passing through underground platforms at Central and Redfern, it surfaces and joins the Illawarra Line at Sydenham. From there, it runs south through major centres like Hurstville and Sutherland. At Sutherland, the line branches, with one branch continuing south to the terminus at Waterfall, and the other heading east on the Cronulla branch line to Cronulla.<sup>21</sup>

**Operational Characteristics:** Serving 33 stations, the T4 line is one of the busiest on the network. It provides the sole rail link to the eastern suburbs and is the primary commuter corridor for the entire southern coastal region of Sydney. The line is notable for its varied geography, transitioning from deep tunnels under the city to at-grade and elevated tracks through the suburbs.<sup>21</sup>

## **T5 Cumberland Line**

The T5 Cumberland Line is unique within the Sydney Trains network as its primary strategic purpose is orbital, not radial. It is designed to connect the major centres of Western Sydney, allowing passengers to travel between the north-west, west, and south-west without needing to travel into and out of the CBD.<sup>23</sup>

**Route Description:** The T5 line forms a large C-shaped arc through the western suburbs. Its southern terminus is Leppington, from where it shares tracks with the T2 and T8 lines to Glenfield and then the T2 and T3 lines to Cabramatta. It continues north through Fairfield and Merrylands before utilizing a critical piece of infrastructure known as the 'Y-link' to connect to the Main Western Line at Harris Park.<sup>23</sup> From there, it travels through Parramatta and Blacktown, with most services terminating at Schofields. Some services, particularly in off-peak periods, extend to Richmond.<sup>23</sup>

**Operational Characteristics:** The creation of the T5 line in 1996 via the Y-link was a deliberate act of transport planning to support Parramatta's role as Sydney's second CBD.<sup>23</sup> By connecting 30 stations across multiple major rail corridors, it facilitates cross-regional journeys and reduces congestion on lines heading into the city. Its service pattern was significantly upgraded in 2017 to include weekend and late-night services, reflecting its growing importance.<sup>23</sup>

## **T6 Lidcombe & Bankstown Line**

The T6 Lidcombe & Bankstown Line is a specialized shuttle service that exists solely due to the ongoing metro conversion of the T3 Bankstown Line. It was created in October 2024 to maintain rail access for a section of the network that would otherwise be isolated.<sup>4</sup>

**Route Description:** The T6 is a short, six-station line. It commences at the major interchange of Lidcombe and travels south on the Main Southern Line to Regents Park, with one intermediate stop at Berala. At Regents Park, it switches to the Bankstown Line, serving Birrong and Yagoona before terminating at Bankstown.<sup>18</sup>

**Operational Characteristics:** This line's function is purely logistical: it acts as a feeder service, connecting the Bankstown area to the broader rail network at Lidcombe and Regents Park. Its existence is temporary and it is expected to be decommissioned or reconfigured once metro services commence along the full length of the line to Bankstown in 2026\.<sup>9</sup>

## **T7 Olympic Park Line**

The T7 Olympic Park Line is another specialized shuttle service, dedicated almost exclusively to serving the Sydney Olympic Park precinct.<sup>26</sup>

**Route Description:** The line consists of just two stations: Lidcombe and Olympic Park. It runs on a short, dedicated branch line that was originally built as the Abattoirs branch and was extensively rebuilt for the Sydney 2000 Olympic Games.<sup>26</sup>

**Operational Characteristics:** Under normal conditions, the T7 operates as a high-frequency shuttle between Lidcombe Platform 0 and Olympic Park, with a journey time of approximately six minutes.<sup>26</sup> During major events at the precinct's stadiums and venues, the service is significantly enhanced, with direct trains running from Central and other major stations to handle large crowds.<sup>27</sup> This makes the T7 a demand-responsive service, unlike the fixed-timetable commuter lines.

## **T8 Airport & South Line**

The T8 Airport & South Line provides a vital link to Sydney's international and domestic airport terminals, while also serving as a major commuter line for the city's south-western suburbs.<sup>29</sup>

**Route Description:** The T8 line originates at Macarthur in the outer south-west and travels north along the Main South Line through major centres like Campbelltown and Glenfield.<sup>29</sup> At a junction near Revesby, the line joins the East Hills Line. It then proceeds towards the city via two distinct routes. The primary route is via the Airport Link tunnel, with privately-operated stations at Green Square, Mascot, Domestic Airport, and International Airport.<sup>29</sup> An alternative, less frequent service pattern bypasses the airport, running via Sydenham. Both routes converge to enter the City Circle.<sup>31</sup>

**Operational Characteristics:** The line serves 33 stations and fulfills a dual role. It is the primary rail access to Sydney Airport for tourists and residents alike, and a key commuter corridor for the Canterbury-Bankstown, Liverpool, and Campbelltown local government areas. The access fee for the airport stations makes it a unique line in terms of its fare structure.<sup>32</sup>

## **T9 Northern Line**

The T9 Northern Line was created in a 2019 network change, effectively reinstating a historic service pattern that connects the northern districts to the CBD via the western line.<sup>4</sup>

**Route Description:** The T9 line commences at Hornsby and travels south along the Main North Line through Epping. At Strathfield, it joins the Main Suburban Line, serving stations like Burwood before reaching Central.<sup>33</sup> From Central, it travels through the City Circle's western limb (Town Hall, Wynyard) and crosses the Harbour Bridge, sharing tracks with the T1 line. It continues up the North Shore Line, terminating at Gordon.<sup>35</sup>

**Operational Characteristics:** Serving 31 stations, the T9 provides a crucial "loop" service, connecting the Northern and North Shore lines via the Strathfield corridor. This offers an alternative route to the city for passengers from the Epping area and provides additional capacity on the heavily trafficked section between Central and the North Shore.<sup>37</sup>

# **III. The Emergence of Sydney Metro (M-Lines)**

## **M1 Metro North West & Bankstown Line**

As of May 2025, the M1 is the only operational metro line in Sydney. Its route from the city's north-west to Sydenham in the inner south demonstrates the system's capacity and technological advantages.<sup>7</sup>

**Route Description:** The operational segment of the M1 line begins at Tallawong in the north-west growth corridor and travels via a mix of elevated viaducts and tunnels through centres like Rouse Hill and Castle Hill to Epping.<sup>38</sup> This initial section, which opened in 2019, incorporated the converted Epping to Chatswood heavy rail line.<sup>1</sup> From the major interchange at Chatswood, the line enters the new City & Southwest tunnels, which opened in August 2024\.<sup>8</sup> It travels under Sydney Harbour to serve new stations in North Sydney (Victoria Cross), the CBD (Barangaroo, Martin Place, Gadigal), and the southern city fringe (Waterloo), before surfacing to connect with new metro platforms at Sydenham station.<sup>5</sup>

**Operational Characteristics:** The M1 line's 21 operational stations are served by Australia's only fully accessible, driverless train service.<sup>39</sup> The defining feature is its high-frequency, "turn-up-and-go" service, with trains every four minutes in peak and every five to ten minutes at other times, providing a seamless travel experience.<sup>6</sup> The introduction of new CBD stations has fundamentally altered commuter patterns, providing direct, high-speed access to key financial, commercial, and recreational precincts that were previously less accessible by rail.<sup>37</sup>

## **Projects Under Construction (Beyond May 2025\)**

While not operational by the specified deadline, three other major metro projects are underway, highlighting the scale of the network's planned expansion.

* **M1 Extension (Sydenham to Bankstown):** The section of the former T3 Bankstown Line between Sydenham and Bankstown was closed in September 2024 for a period of up to 12 months to facilitate its conversion to metro standards.<sup>40</sup> This project involves upgrading 10 existing stations to be fully accessible and equipping them with platform screen doors.<sup>9</sup> Metro services are expected to extend to Bankstown in 2026, at which point the line will be complete.<sup>8</sup>  
* **Sydney Metro West:** This is a separate, fully underground metro line that will connect the Greater Parramatta area with the Sydney CBD. Stations are confirmed for Westmead, Parramatta, Sydney Olympic Park, and Hunter Street in the city, among others.<sup>42</sup> This project is a massive undertaking designed to double the rail capacity between Sydney's two largest CBDs. Construction is well underway, with a target opening date of 2032\.<sup>44</sup>  
* **Sydney Metro – Western Sydney Airport:** This new 23 km line will provide the foundational public transport link to the new Western Sydney International (Nancy-Bird Walton) Airport.<sup>46</sup> It will connect the airport to the existing Sydney Trains network at St Marys station. The line is scheduled to open concurrently with the airport, with current projections indicating a start of services in 2026 or early 2027\.<sup>47</sup>
