### Broader Musical Influences in Parts 4–6

Araki's integration of music isn't arbitrary; it enhances thematic resonance. For instance, Pink Floyd's appearances in Part 4 underscore the arc's focus on community and hidden threats in Morioh, with their atmospheric sound evoking mystery. Queen's "Killer Queen" perfectly encapsulates Kira's villainy, blending elegance with destruction—much like the band's operatic flair. Bob Dylan's introspective style fits Rohan's creative yet intrusive Stand, while The Rolling Stones' raw energy matches Bucciarati's gritty determination in Italy's underworld.

Beyond these five, other potential 1970s nods in Parts 4–6 include Bad Company (Part 4, after the 1974 song/album by Bad Company) and Highway to Hell (Part 6, after AC/DC's 1979 song), but these were excluded to prioritize direct song/album matches without band-name ambiguity. ### Cultural and Historical Context

The 1970s rock scene, post-Beatles breakup, saw experimentation with longer tracks (e.g., "Echoes" at over 23 minutes) and conceptual albums, paralleling JoJo's evolving Stand mechanics. Pink Floyd's Wish You Were Here (1975), containing "Shine On You Crazy Diamond," deals with absence and madness—themes echoed in Josuke's family dynamics. Queen's Sheer Heart Attack (1974) album, home to "Killer Queen," marked their breakthrough, much like Kira's arc as a breakout antagonist.

Dylan's "Knockin' on Heaven's Door," written for the 1973 film Pat Garrett and Billy the Kid, adds meta-layers, as JoJo often blends Western influences. The Rolling Stones' Sticky Fingers, with its controversial zipper cover by Andy Warhol, embodies the era's boundary-pushing art, akin to Araki's bold visuals.

Fan discussions, such as on Reddit and YouTube analyses (e.g., Hamon Beat's videos), consistently affirm these links, with artbooks like JOJO A-GO!GO! providing visual confirmations. While some names are altered in English dubs/localizations (e.g., Sticky Fingers to Zipper Man) to avoid trademarks, the originals retain their musical essence.

This survey underscores how 1970s music not only names but infuses depth into JoJo's world, making these parts enduring favorites for their blend of action, eccentricity, and rock homage.
