Tom Taylor's contributions to DC Comics have significantly shaped alternate universe storytelling, particularly through his ability to blend high-stakes drama with character-driven narratives. As the author of Injustice: Gods Among Us Year One, Taylor established a benchmark for exploring moral ambiguities in superhero lore, where Batman's unwavering principles often clash with escalating conflicts. This series, first released in 2013, serves as a prequel to the popular video game, depicting a world where Superman's grief leads to authoritarian rule, and Batman emerges as the leader of a resistance movement. The complete collection compiles the initial year's issues, offering readers a comprehensive entry into this dystopian saga. For DC fans, it represents a pivotal exploration of heroism's darker sides, with Batman's tactical genius and ethical stance at the forefront.

Building on this foundation, Taylor's other works demonstrate his versatility while consistently incorporating Batman in meaningful roles. One such series is DCeased, launched in 2019, which introduces a techno-organic virus that ravages Earth, infecting billions and transforming them into rage-fueled undead. Batman plays a crucial part early on, using his intellect to analyze the outbreak and coordinate with other heroes like Superman and Wonder Woman in a desperate bid for survival. The story delves into themes of loss, sacrifice, and the fragility of human (and superhuman) connections, with Batman's arc involving poignant moments of vulnerability, such as protecting his family amid chaos. Critics and fans alike have noted how Taylor's writing infuses horror elements with emotional weight, making DCeased a standout for those seeking a blend of action and heartbreak. The graphic novel compilation provides a self-contained read, though expansions like DCeased: Dead Planet and DCeased: Unkillables extend the universe, further featuring Batman's legacy through characters like Damian Wayne.

In a different vein, Dark Knights of Steel transports the DC pantheon to a medieval fantasy world, where prophecies and ancient rivalries drive the plot. Released starting in 2021, this series reimagines Batman as a knightly figure in the Kingdom of El, entangled in intrigues involving Kal-El's arrival from the stars. Batman's detective prowess shines as he uncovers secrets that threaten multiple kingdoms, including alliances and betrayals among familiar heroes recast in feudal roles—such as Harley Quinn as a jester or Green Arrow as an archer outlaw. Taylor's narrative weaves elements of Arthurian legend with superhero tropes, creating a rich tapestry that explores power dynamics and destiny. While the series has sparked discussions on its innovative world-building, some readers appreciate how it humanizes Batman beyond his modern gadgets, focusing on his strategic mind in a sword-and-sorcery context. The ongoing expansions, like Dark Knights of Steel: Allwinter, continue to build on this foundation, maintaining Batman's centrality in epic confrontations.

Complementing these larger-scale tales is Batman: The Detective, a 2021 mini-series that shifts to a more intimate, globe-trotting thriller. Here, Batman ventures outside Gotham to confront Equilibrium, a group targeting individuals he's saved in the past, drawing him into a personal vendetta across Europe. Written by Taylor with art by Andy Kubert, the story emphasizes Batman's role as the world's greatest detective, incorporating elements of mentorship, legacy, and international alliances. Encounters with figures like Knight and Squire add layers to Batman's network, while the plot's twists highlight his adaptability in unfamiliar territories. This series stands out for its grounded approach compared to Taylor's other alternate realities, offering a character study that resonates with fans of classic Batman mysteries. Collected in a single volume, it provides a concise yet impactful read that underscores Taylor's skill in crafting Batman-centric stories without relying on ensemble casts.
